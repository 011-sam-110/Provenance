# Landing page sources

Every figure on `/` that was not measured by this repo, with the source it came from and the
sentence it was read out of. The page argues that its numbers are checkable. This is where a
reader checks them.

Figures the repo measures itself are not listed here — they come from
`lib/marketing/coverage-audit.data.ts`, which is generated by `scripts/gen-landing-audit.mjs`
from a production run of `scripts/country-event-breakdown.mts` and carries its own measurement
date.

Last verified: 2026-09-12. Every URL below was opened on that date.

## Public money

### €5.421 billion — Copernicus, 2021 to 2027

- **Source:** Regulation (EU) 2021/696 of the European Parliament and of the Council,
  *Official Journal of the European Union* L 170, 12 May 2021.
- **URL:** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2021/696/oj
- **Locator:** Article 11(1), OJ L 170/96–97.
- **Quote:**
  > The financial envelope for the implementation of the Programme for the period from
  > 1 January 2021 to 31 December 2027 and for covering the associated risks shall be
  > EUR 14,880 billion in current prices.
  >
  > […] (b) for Copernicus: EUR 5,421 billion;
- **Note on notation:** the Official Journal uses a comma as the decimal separator, so
  "EUR 5,421 billion" is €5.421 billion, not €5,421 billion. The page writes it as €5.421bn.

### The data it buys must be given back free

- **Source:** as above.
- **Locator:** Article 53(1), Chapter III "Copernicus data policy".
- **Quote:**
  > Copernicus data and Copernicus information shall be provided to Copernicus users under
  > the following free, full and open data policy: (a) Copernicus users may, on a free and
  > worldwide basis, reproduce, distribute, communicate to the public, adapt, and modify all
  > Copernicus data and Copernicus information and combine them with other data and
  > information;
- **Scope:** this is a statutory obligation on the EU programme. It says nothing about any
  other country's data, and the page does not extend it to one.

### $137.4 billion — government space spending, 2025

- **Source:** Novaspace, *Government Space Programs*, 25th edition. Press release,
  20 January 2026.
- **URL:** https://nova.space/press-release/global-space-spending-reaches-137b-marking-a-defense-led-era/
- **Quote:** "$137.4B government space market in 2025"
- **Split:** defence $73.5B (54%), civil $63.7B.
- **Scope:** all government space spending, not Earth observation alone. The press release
  gives no Earth-observation-specific figure, so the page does not claim one.

### $2.195 billion — NASA Earth Science, FY 2025

- **Source:** NASA, *FY 2025 NASA Spending Plan*.
- **URL:** https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/fy-2025-spend-plan-march-2026.pdf
- **Quote (line items, $ millions):**
  > NASA 24,807.1 / SCIENCE 7,332.2 / Earth Science 2,195.0

## Surveillance

All camera figures come from one study, at one scope. See the scope warning in
`lib/marketing/surveillance.data.ts` before adding another.

- **Source:** Comparitech, *Surveillance Camera Statistics: Which City has the Most CCTV?*
- **URL:** https://www.comparitech.com/vpn-privacy/the-worlds-most-surveilled-cities/
- **Updated:** 25 June 2025.
- **Scope:** "public CCTV—cameras used by government entities such as law enforcement", across
  the 150 most populated cities in the world.

### The ranking used on the page

| Rank | Place | Cameras | Population | Per 1,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cities of China\* | 700,000,000 | 1,420,000,000 | 494.25 |
| 2 | Hyderabad, India | 900,000 | 11,337,900 | 79.38 |
| 3 | Indore, India | 251,500 | 3,482,830 | 72.21 |
| 4 | Bangalore, India | 585,284 | 14,395,400 | 40.66 |
| 5 | Lahore, Pakistan | 410,297 | 14,825,800 | 27.67 |

\* Not a city measurement. **Quote:** "We have used this ratio to create estimated CCTV camera
figures per Chinese city" — the study divided 700 million by China's population and applied
the national ratio to each city. The page flags this on the row itself.

### The reputation check

- London: "13.4 cameras per 1,000 people", over 130,000 public cameras.
- Los Angeles: "46,766 cameras, which equates to 12.4 cameras per 1,000 people."
- New York City: "80,303 cameras to a population of just over 7.9 million (10.12 cameras per
  1,000 people)."

### Averages

- **Quote:** "Previously, we measured an average of 4.31 cameras per 1,000 people (123.74 per
  1,000 people when including China), while today there are 5.82 cameras per 1,000 people
  (139.96 per 1,000 people when including China)."

### The study's own caveat, quoted on the page

- **Quote:** "Due to a wide range of sources reporting estimates and a general lack of public
  information regarding CCTV cameras, actual figures may be higher or lower than what is
  indicated."

## Figures that were REMOVED, and why

Keeping this list matters more than the list above. Each of these was on a draft of the page
and could not be supported.

### "$29,000,000,000 spent globally creating the infrastructure to monitor the world"

No source carries this sentence. The nearest real figure is Omdia's video surveillance market
size, reported as "The global video surveillance market grew by 4.3 per cent to $27 billion in
2025"
([Cyprus Mail, 19 August 2026](https://cyprus-mail.com/2026/08/19/global-video-surveillance-market-reaches-27-billion-as-ai-reshapes-sector);
Omdia's own release returns HTTP 403 to automated fetches). That figure fails the sentence
twice:

1. It is **annual revenue for one year**, not cumulative spend on building anything.
2. It is **overwhelmingly private spending** — the source gives no public/private split — so it
   cannot support "you already paid for this".

Replaced with the public budgets above, which are public money and can be quoted as such.

### The country-level CCTV ranking

The draft ranked South Korea ~529, China ~490–500, the UK ~304–309, the US ~244–256 and
Vietnam ~100–150 cameras per 1,000 people. Only the China figure traces to a source. The others
are total-installed-base estimates (every camera including private ones) divided by population,
placed in a table beside a public-CCTV figure. Comparitech measures London at **13.4** per
1,000, so the draft's UK figure was roughly 23 times the study's, for a different thing.

Replaced with the city ranking above, which is one study at one scope.

### "2,212 unit tests across 256 files"

Wrong at the time it was written. `CLAUDE.md`, measured 2026-09-08 with `npx vitest list`,
records **3,677 cases across 359 files**. The page now reads the figure from the repo rather
than restating it.
